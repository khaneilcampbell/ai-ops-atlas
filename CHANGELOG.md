# Changelog

What changed in Atlas, newest first. Every pull request that changes the app adds one line at the
top of the list: `- YYYY-MM-DD · What changed, in plain words (#PR)`. The newest five lines are
shown in the Mac app's **Update available** button, so write them for KC, not for a reviewer.
- 2026-11-01 · Zooming in close to a building no longer turns the map dark at night or washed out by day: the distance haze now stays out past the neighbourhood you are looking at (#PR)
- 2026-11-01 · Behind the scenes: every map change is now checked against a performance budget, and the screenshot check has more time so it stops getting cut off
- 2026-10-31 · If the 3D map ever fails to draw, the rest of the Projects view stays and a Try again button restarts the map, instead of the whole page crashing
- 2026-10-31 · Airport jets now look like large Boeing-style airliners: long slim body, swept wings, big underwing engines and a swept tail, still in Delta, Emirates, American, Southwest and Qatar colours (#164)
- 2026-10-31 · Airport offices and hangars now look like Atlas HQ (blue glass bands, white fins, gold crown), and every airport building is 1.5x taller to stand with the Financial District, Marketplace and Marketing Agency (#164)
- 2026-10-31 · Airport front entrance: a curved canopy over the kerb, revolving glass doors, the sign on the canopy and a zebra crossing; a landside plaza now joins the airport to the district across from it, and the airport buildings use Atlas HQ's blue, gold and white (#164)
- 2026-10-31 · Ground crew now work outside at the airport: fuel trucks, bag loaders and baggage trains at the parked jets, a cargo tug, crew walking out from the terminal and a marshaller at the runway hold point. They never go near the runway
- 2026-10-31 · Atlas Airport has a new terminal: an oval glass hall under a ribbed white roof with a green glass skylight, a glass pier with rounded ends, a twisting control tower, an oval car park and painted rings round every gate
- 2026-10-31 · Planes take off like real jets: they speed up down the runway, lift the nose, climb gradually, fold their wheels away and bank into a smooth turn. Jets now fly in Delta, Emirates, American, Southwest and Qatar Airways colours

The same lobby design is there when you enter the building, west of Navy Federal and Vanguard by the highway. The garden-balcony tower is now called Garden Residences (#156)
- 2026-10-31 · BMW moved next to the Financial District on the waterfront, and the Hills train now runs behind the towers to a new stop in Financial Park. Two groups of six commuters now walk from different homes at their own pace to catch the train, which waits 15 seconds; some go to Circa, American Express and the Ritz-Carlton, Ritz guests return to the mansion and one returns to Vista House. Financial Park station now has a Dubai-style shell roof and a glass skybridge down to the park, and the train is a bullet train. Financial Park is now a central green with a stepped fountain, sculpture gardens and flower beds. Financial Park has palm trees, and the roads beside it are tidied: the highway ramp leaves the district street at a clean corner and airport trucks drive on the street instead of a second road squeezed beside it BMW is now a slim glass tower sized like the Financial District towers, with car galleries on every floor. At night every building now shows lights: a floodlit stadium with glowing scoreboards, BMW showroom ceilings glowing white on every floor, windows on all sides of the Skyline shops, and lit windows on the Hills garage and airport service building (#158)

Highway rails and edge lines no longer show up on city streets, and the airport truck shares the ramp road beside Vanguard instead of a second tangled road. Road bridges between districts are now paved with the same asphalt and center line as the streets, so each street runs straight across instead of turning into pale stone. Junctions on the Financial District's main street are now one clean intersection each, with no curbs, walks or doubled center lines crossing them, and zebra crossings where side roads cross the walks (#165)
